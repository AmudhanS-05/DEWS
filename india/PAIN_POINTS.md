# Why open Indian data cannot yet feed a flash-flood warning pipeline like ours: pain points

Decision (team, 1 Nov 2026): the India study stops at this analysis. We do not run the pipeline on Indian data; we
list what we met when we tried to assemble the Indian parallel of each US source, why it is not comparable, and what it
would cost a warning. `india/replay_india.py` (step B replay) remains a tool for later, not run on real data.

Evidence used, and how strong it is:
- **Observed**: our India-WRIS API runs from the user's laptop on an Indian connection. `--discover` (31 Oct 2026, 11
  combinations) and `--list-stations` (1 Nov 2026: July 2022, agency CWC, 41 basin/tributary and state/district
  combinations, 83 stations found; the station list is on the laptop, not in git).
- **Measured**: our degradation experiment on REAL US data (step C, `india/figures/degradation.csv`,
  `python -m india.degrade`). 38 USGS gauges, 2024-2026, 64 official minor-stage crossings. The 3 h rise-rate rule
  detects 0.52 of them at US data quality (the trained s3_v0 model detects 0.45). Each row below changes one property.
- **Unverified**: anything marked so. These are stated from documentation or general knowledge and were not checked
  in this study.

| # | Pain point | What we observed in India | US counterpart we used | Why it is not comparable | Effect on flash-flood warning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Real-time access and feed delay** | India-WRIS serves historical records through a POST API; no live feed was found. CWC's real-time flood forecasts exist, but as web pages and daily PDF bulletins, not as a data API (observed on the portal; their latency was not measured). | USGS NWIS Instantaneous Values API: readings appear about 24 to 69 min after measurement (one live sample, FAQ.md). | Our producer polls an API every few minutes. India-WRIS gives the past, not the present, and CWC's real-time products are documents meant for people to read. | Measured on US data: a 1 h feed delay cuts detection from 0.52 to 0.12, 6 h to 0.05, 24 h to 0.02. Without a live feed the warning arrives after the crossing. |
| 2 | **Reporting interval, and how it changes with season and station** | July 2022: most CWC river stations about every 1 h (744 records in 31 days). Dams and reservoirs every 3 to 24 h (Karanja, Malaprabha, Musi, Pulichintala 24 h; Idamalayar, Idukki 3 h; Bawanthadi, Pench 6 h). Some stations only 12 to 32 records in the month. Neeleeswaram's February 2023 records were 08:00 readings. Off-season intervals unverified. | USGS IV every 5 to 15 min, all year (median 5 min across our 38 gauges, measured). | Our windows, rise rates and models assume several readings per hour. Hourly data turns each window into one point, and the interval varies by station and season. | Measured on US data: hourly readings cut detection from 0.52 to 0.17, 3-hourly to 0.14, daily (the dams/off-season case) to 0.05. The trained s3_v0 raises no alerts at all on hourly data. On one real flood (BDAT2, 4 Jul 2025) the warning shrinks from 1.5 h at 15 min to 0.5 h at hourly (synthetic replay, `india/figures/ideal_india_replay.csv`). |
| 3 | **Manual readings vs telemetry** | Three method descriptions in one month: "MANUAL-Water Level by Staff Gauge (0)", "MANUAL-WL by Staff Gauge (MSL)", "INSAT-WL by Telemetry". | Automated sensors with telemetry, and quality codes (P provisional, A approved) on every reading. | A manual reading depends on an observer being present, which is least certain during a flood at night. Manual readings have no provisional/approved flag to separate checked values from raw ones. Timing (on the hour, or 08:00) follows a duty roster, not the event. | Not measured per station. Expected: missed or late readings at the peak; the gap result below gives a floor on the cost. |
| 4 | **Vertical datum** | Two datums in the same dataset: gauge zero ("(0)") and mean sea level ("(MSL)"). | One local gauge datum per gauge, documented by USGS, consistent with the NWPS flood stages. | Thresholds and readings must be on the same datum; mixing them shifts levels by the gauge's zero elevation. The API gives the datum only inside the free-text description. | Expected: a threshold on the wrong datum makes alerts impossible or constant. Not measured. |
| 5 | **Flood thresholds not machine-readable** | The API returns no warning or danger levels. CWC levels (warning, danger, highest flood level) appear in web pages and daily bulletins and must be copied by hand (`india/thresholds.csv` is a template). | NWPS API: action, minor, moderate and major stages as numbers for all 38 gauges. | Our labels are official categories by design. Without them, either someone transcribes them per station, or we estimate them, and an estimate is not an official level. | Measured on US data, using a percentile of the gauge's own record in place of the official level: 99.9th percentile gives detection 0.41 with 2.82 instead of 1.95 false alarms per gauge-year; 99.5th gives 0.23 with 5.42. |
| 6 | **Rainfall: no MRMS equivalent** | India-WRIS has a rainfall dataset (not fetched). IMD's gridded rainfall is daily at 0.25 degrees; GPM IMERG is half-hourly at 0.1 degrees with latency of hours (Early run) to months (Final). Specifics unverified in this study. | MRMS: 1 km grid, radar QPE every 2 min; hourly products about 3-5 min (radar-only), 16 min (Pass 1) and 58 min (Pass 2) after the hour (measured, FAQ.md); public S3 archive from Oct 2020. | A 10 km circle around a gauge holds about 294 MRMS cells but less than one 0.25-degree cell. Daily totals cannot show a storm's timing within the day. | In our own US evaluation rain did not improve the forecaster (ml/MODEL_CARD.md), so this is not the binding constraint for our models. It matters for any rain-driven forecast. |
| 7 | **Discovery: no station catalogue** | No endpoint lists stations. We found 83 by scanning 41 basin/tributary and state/district combinations for one month. Several returned nothing (Godavari/Indravati, Wardha, Penganga, Krishna/Ghataprabha, Kerala/Alappuzha, Telangana/Jayashankar Bhupalpally). The basin name used as tributaryName returns nothing. | NWIS site service and the NWPS gauge list: every gauge with ID, coordinates and categories, searchable. | You cannot know what exists without guessing names; an empty answer does not tell "no station" from "wrong spelling". | Coverage is unknown, so a fair comparison per basin is not possible. |
| 8 | **API reliability and documentation** | HTTP 200 with `{"statusCode": 500, "message": "No data found..."}`. tributaryName effectively required. Page size at most 1000. Response schema undocumented (OpenAPI says only StatusHandler{statusCode, message, data}). Field names learned from responses. | Documented REST services (WaterML-JSON, NWPS JSON) with stable schemas and normal HTTP status codes. | Errors cannot be told from empty results by HTTP status, and the parser had to be written from samples. | Engineering cost and silent-failure risk; not a skill number. |
| 9 | **Geo-restriction** | The API was unreachable from our cloud session (DNS / connection refused). Reachable from an Indian connection. | USGS, NWPS and MRMS reachable worldwide without a key. | Any processing outside India (cloud, collaborators abroad) cannot fetch the data directly. | Operational and reproducibility cost. |
| 10 | **Reservoir regulation** | Many listed stations are dams and reservoirs (Idukki, Idamalayar, Pulichintala, Pench, ...), reporting every 3 to 24 h. | Our 38 US gauges are river gauges; some sit on regulated rivers (e.g. Colorado River at Austin), but all report at 5 to 15 min. | A reservoir level reflects operating decisions, not only inflow; at 3 to 24 h it cannot show a flash flood's rise. | Expected: unusable for flash-flood timing; useful for release information instead. |
| 11 | **Licensing and terms** | Terms of use for India-WRIS data not checked in this study (unverified). CAMELS-IND reports that only 645 of 4,824 listed gauges are free to use. | USGS data are public domain; NWPS and MRMS are public US government data. | Unclear terms limit redistribution, including publishing a replay dataset with the paper. | Legal and reproducibility cost; not measured. |
| 12 | **Gaps** | Some stations had 12 to 32 records in July 2022 where hourly would be 744. Gap statistics per station are not measured (the fetch was not run, by decision). | USGS: median 0.8 % of readings missing across our gauges; median longest gap 120 h (measured, Table 1). | Gaps in a manual or telemetry record during a monsoon month are the gaps that matter most. | Measured on US data: 10 % of readings removed gives detection 0.48, 30 % gives 0.39 (from 0.52); far smaller than the interval and delay effects. |

What an Indian station would need to match the US numbers, from the same experiment
(`india/figures/ideal_india_requirements.md`): readings as frequent as the US gauges (hourly is already too coarse), a
feed delay below 1 h (1 h already loses most detections; nothing finer was tested), at most about 10 % missing
readings, and official machine-readable thresholds. Points 1, 2 and 5 are the binding ones. Points 3, 4 and 7 to 11
make the data hard to use even before the forecast. Point 6 limits rain-driven methods, not our stage-based ones.
